The rotation, by Tuesday of the month

  1. 1st
    Postings and people. Read every new posting at UBC and SFU and log it on The Role. One relationship begun or kept warm. The monthly pipeline review.
  2. 2nd
    Ship something. One resource edited toward publication, or published and deposited. See Contribute.
  3. 3rd
    The landscape. One node improved: a status corrected, a case study attached, a practised-in line confirmed.
  4. 4th
    The adjudication gap. One move from The Role: a conversation, the interactive-process resource, a route into a unit.
  5. 5th
    Calls and the calendar. Calls for proposals, committees, a talk pitched. The rolling list on the Calendar moved into dates.

The other six days

  1. 15 min
    Until the exam: ten CPACC practice questions or the flashcards, any time of day. Spacing is what a recall exam rewards, and one long Tuesday cannot supply it.
  2. One
    One commitment outside Tuesday, never two: crisis-line training now; the documentation-facing volunteer role from the spring.

The rule of scale is two commitments beside the job. Studio Day runs at full intensity in 2026–27, so Sundays are not available to borrow. When the plan is bigger than the Tuesdays, something is sequenced, never squeezed.

For the days it gets heavy: a student who got the access they needed without having to ask twice. Every ledger on this site is a way of making that happen more often.

    Why this stands

    The shape of the week

    One Tuesday, ten until six, about 7.5 working hours, and fifteen minutes on the other days until the CPACC is passed. From 22 September 2026 to the start of the MSc that is about 45 working Tuesdays, or about 340 hours. The plans as first written asked for about 500. The difference was closed by sequencing, not cutting: the corpus study is piloted and preregistered before Leeds and finished in the first MSc year, Fieldwork goes fortnightly until the exam, and two of the four resources ship before the application and two after.

    The governing principle

    One question, one flagship, one method, one front door. The question is the one written for Leeds: how learning design in education and work decides who is included. The flagship is the essential-requirements corpus study. The method is Fieldwork. The front door is accessiblelearninglabs.com. Everything else is a product of those four, or a private plan behind them.

    Plans private, products public

    This repository is a plan. It names the offices being applied to and the route in, so it stays private. What leaves it goes out as a product: a resource with a DOI, a preregistration, a paper, a practised-in line on the landscape. The Contribute page is the door between the two.

    What excellence is

    Not more things. Fewer things, finished, dated, public, and made with disabled people rather than about them. The Ceiling sets it out goal by goal.

    Where this sits among the four properties

    Accessible Learning Labs is the specialism: learning design at the intersection of accessibility, universal design for learning and disability justice. It runs at getting-started intensity in 2026–27 while Studio Day runs at full, becomes full intensity in 2027–29 because the MSc is Access Lab, and drops to its floor while the counselling master's runs. A degree is not a pause in a property. It is that property at full intensity.
