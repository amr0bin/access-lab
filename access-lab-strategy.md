# Access Lab · Strategy

*Working note, 19 September 2026. Strategy, direction, and what excellence could be, across the three goals — the CPACC, the Leeds MSc, an Accessibility Advisor role at UBC or SFU — and across the five places the work now lives: accessiblelearninglabs.com, the Fieldwork repository, the accessibility-advisor-eligibility repository, the disability-studies repository, and this project. Written after reading all three repositories in full and the site's public pages. Governs how the pieces fit together; `access-lab.md` governs the weekly running of it; `00-portfolio-spine-shared.md` governs anything across properties.*

## 1 · The short version

1. **The thinking is already excellent. The system is not yet visible as one.** The eligibility plan, the landscape evidence, the corpus-study reference and the nine-essays map are each better than most people in this field ever write down. They live in five places, speak in three voices, and a reader who finds one cannot see the others. Access Lab's job is to make them one practice with one question.
2. **One question, one flagship, one method, one front door.** The question is the one already written for Leeds: *how learning design in education and work decides who is included.* The flagship is the essential-requirements corpus study. The method is Fieldwork. The front door is accessiblelearninglabs.com. Everything else is either a product of those four or a private plan behind them.
3. **Plans private, products public.** Two of the three repositories are strategy documents, and they are public. Move them behind the door before the next posting appears. Section 5.
4. **The plan is about 50% bigger than the Tuesdays that carry it.** Roughly 500 hours of named work against roughly 340 hours of Tuesdays before September 2027. Something has to slow, and it should be chosen now. Section 6.
5. **Excellence here is not more things. It is fewer things finished, dated, public, and made with disabled people rather than about them.** Section 2 says what that looks like, goal by goal.

## 2 · What excellence could be

Three levels for each goal. *Met* is what the gate requires. *Strong* is what a well-prepared candidate brings. *Excellent* is what makes a reader remember the file. The excellent column is the target; the met column is the floor that must never slip.

### The CPACC

| Met | Strong | Excellent |
|---|---|---|
| Passed | Passed first attempt, on the twelve-week plan, before the Leeds application goes in | Passed, then *used*: the IAAP community joined, the continuing-education credits earned through work already planned (talks, the corpus study), and the credential placed on the CV where the postings' knowledge lines sit |

**Do not over-invest.** Neither advisor posting names the CPACC, and your own eligibility plan says it closes most of the knowledge lines. Its value is a dated, external, legible marker at low cost. There is no prize for a high score. Twelve weeks, then stop.

### Leeds

| | Met | Strong | Excellent |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Admission** | Entry route satisfied; statement; referees | A clear question; a statement that reads as a practitioner who has already started | Applying as a practitioner-researcher: a preprint or a preregistered study on OSF, a published resource, referees who have read the work, a Snowdon grant application, and a named partner for the Applied Project |
| **The programme** | Nine modules and the project passed | Every assessment takes one angle on the one question (the rule in `nine-essays-one-question.md`) | Every assessment also becomes a public artifact within a month of submission; the Applied Project runs with ethics approval, a partner organisation and disabled co-researchers; the degree finishes at the level the doctorate route needs (typically a distinction; **verify** Leeds's classification rules and what the doctoral routes in the spine actually require) |

**The excellent version is mostly already designed.** `nine-essays-one-question.md` is exactly what the best part-time master's students do and rarely write down. What lifts it from strong to excellent is conversion: a module essay that becomes a practitioner guide one weekend later is two ledger entries, and after two years it is a body of published work that happened to also earn a degree.

### The Accessibility Advisor role

| Met | Strong | Excellent |
|---|---|---|
| Shortlisted on the equivalence clause | Hired into the unit at any level, per your own Move 1; the advisor role follows from inside | Hired as someone the unit already knows — from a committee, a conversation, a CADSPPE session, or a resource they already use — whose file answers the panel's one doubt (*has she decided accommodations?*) before it is asked, and who has published on the problem the office finds hardest |

**The hardest problem in those offices is where an accommodation request meets an essential requirement.** It is the call that ends up at tribunal, the one advisors describe as most difficult, and the one a candidate can most easily show thinking about in public. The corpus study, the four-literature paper and landscape node ac6 are all about exactly this. That is why the corpus study is the flagship (Section 4).

**Excellence in the role itself,** once held, is the advisor whose design work reduces the number of individual accommodations needed, without ever treating individual accommodation as lesser. The site already says this well: *individual accommodation is a right and a skilled professional practice.* Keep that sentence and carry it into Fieldwork, where the hypothesis currently reads as setting disability justice against individual accommodation. A panel for an accommodation job will read both.

### Excellence at the level of the field

What distinguishes the people in Canadian post-secondary accessibility whom others cite:

- **They own a question in public.** Not a topic. A question with a dated trail of answers. Yours is written; it needs to appear on the front door.
- **Their resources are used by other institutions.** Adoption is the evidence. CC BY or CC BY-SA, deposited with DOIs, versioned. This is why the licence matters more here than in any other property.
- **They sit where accommodation meets design.** Most practitioners are on one side: advisors who do casework, or designers who do UDL. The rare ones hold both, with the law and the measurement reasoning between them. That intersection is your positioning claim, and the essential-requirements work is its proof.
- **They are accountable to disabled people, visibly.** Co-authors, paid readers, named contributors. Not a values statement; a contributor log.
- **They are in the professional rooms**: CADSPPE, AHEAD, CDSA, an Accessible BC Act committee, JPED and CJDS. One of each is enough to be known. None of them requires an affiliation.

**What ties them together** is not a new site. A panel member who arrives anywhere can find the rest in one click.

## 4 · The flagship: the essential-requirements corpus study

If only one piece of independent work gets done well before September 2027, it should be this one, because it is the only piece that serves all three goals and the consulting offer at once.

| It serves | Because |
|---|---|
| **The advisor role** | It is public, methodical reasoning about the exact decision advisors find hardest: when a requirement is bona fide and when it is inherited convention (*Meiorin*). It answers the panel's doubt about adjudication without claiming casework you have not done |
| **Leeds** | It turns the application from a practitioner's into a practitioner-researcher's; it becomes the sampling frame for the Applied Project; it feeds the Global Challenges and Building Enabling Futures essays, as the nine-essays map already says |
| **The CPACC** | Domain three (standards, laws, management strategies) in applied form. A small overlap, but real |
| **The consulting offer** | A programme asking *are our standards defensible?* can be answered with comparative data |
| **The ethic** | The second coder can be a disabled practitioner, paid and offered co-authorship. That same person can be Access Lab's named reader. One relationship, two roles, both honouring *nothing about us without us* |

**Which paper comes first is settled by your own later reasoning.** The spine and the setup pack say the three-pillars paper precedes the corpus study; `corpus-study-and-conceptual-paper-reference.md` (7 September) argues, correctly, that the seven codes trace to the four literatures, so the **four-literature framework paper** precedes the corpus study and the three-pillars paper belongs to Fieldwork on its own timetable. Adopt the 7 September recommendation and record it in the spine's open decisions so the two documents stop disagreeing.

**The forcing function is preregistration.** Set the OSF preregistration date as a calendar entry the moment the pilot twenty are coded. It is the deadline an institution would otherwise supply.

## 5 · Fix the public surface first

A panel member, a Leeds reader or a prospective client who searches your name will find these. Each is small. Together they are the difference between a practice that looks considered and one that looks unfinished.

**Urgent, before the next posting appears**

1. **Make `accessibility-advisor-eligibility` private.** It is excellent strategy and it is a job-search playbook in public: it names the units you are targeting, describes how to become an internal candidate, discusses shortlisting and age, and sets out "the honest odds." A panel member at UBC or SFU who reads it is reading your tactics about their office. Keep it; hide it. Move to a public place only what is written as a product: the landscape evidence becomes practised-in lines on the map, and the postings analysis becomes the public piece your own plan proposes (*What seven BC universities ask of the people who decide accommodation*).
2. **Make `disability-studies` private, or split it.** `nine-essays-one-question.md` and the corpus-study reference are plans; the public versions are the preregistration and the papers. Standpoint statements in them ("written from inside the population") are yours to disclose where and when you choose, and a public planning repository is not a place you chose deliberately.
3. **Add a licence to the Fieldwork repository.** Without a LICENSE file the default is all rights reserved, which contradicts the CC BY / CC BY-SA decision in `record-and-rights-across-the-portfolio.md`. Say which licence covers text and which covers templates.

**Before the Leeds application**

4. **Bring Fieldwork's legal floor to BC.** The repository cites the ACA, AODA and ADA and does not mention the Accessible British Columbia Act, which is the law UBC and SFU plan under. It also cites WCAG 2.1 throughout; WCAG 2.2 has been the current W3C Recommendation since October 2023. Update, or state why 2.1 is the reference.
5. **Repair the repository's links and claims.** 37 internal links point to pages that do not exist (weeks 10–24 as separate pages; concept pages for WCAG, UDL and methodology that were merged). The README says it is updated weekly; the last commit is 17 July 2026; the case archive shows weeks 7–24 as "to be documented." Either update it or say "updated as the series is published." Settle 24-week versus 48-week between the README and `_config.yml`.
6. **Resolve "fifteen years."** It appears six times across the two repositories. The house rule is "over the past decade." Pick one before any CV goes out.
7. **Confirm the ICBC "ADA-aligned" line** your landscape evidence already flags. An accessibility panel in BC will notice ADA used for Canadian work.
8. **Put the question and the record on the front door.** The site does not yet mention the essential-requirements study, the CPACC in progress, or the Leeds route, and has no ORCID link. One short *Current inquiry* section fixes all three.

## 6 · The honest arithmetic

Tuesdays from 22 September 2026 to the start of the MSc: 50. Allowing for about five lost to illness, holidays and life: about 45 working Tuesdays at 7.5 hours, or **about 340 hours**.

What the current plans ask of those Tuesdays, estimated:

| Work | Hours (estimate) |
|---|---|
| CPACC, including the off-day recall | 72 |
| Fieldwork, one move a week | 60 |
| Corpus study, per its own plan (4–6 h a week for 8–10 months) | 160–260; call it 200 |
| The four written resources, edited and shipped | 80 |
| Leeds application | 20 |
| CV rebuild, equivalence paragraph, applications | 30 |
| Informational conversations, committee | 20 |
| The record and the rotation | 22 |
| **Total** | **about 500** |

That is about 160 hours over, before any volunteering, the crisis line, Make Space or the counselling prerequisites, all of which fall outside Tuesdays and compete for the same week.

**Three ways to close it, in order of preference:**

1. **Let the corpus study run into the first MSc year.** Pilot, preregister and code the first twenty before September 2027; finish coding and write the paper alongside Doing Disability Research, where it belongs anyway. The Leeds application then carries a *preregistered study*, which is a strong and honest claim, rather than a rushed preprint. Saves about 100 hours before September.
2. **Move Fieldwork to fortnightly** until the CPACC is passed, then weekly again. A fortnightly series is not a stalled one. Saves about 10 hours, and more importantly protects the CPACC Tuesdays from the one routine most likely to expand.
3. **Ship two resources before the application, two after.** Your own competitiveness plan already stages them this way. Saves about 40 hours.

Together those bring the year to about 350, within a lost Tuesday or two of what is available. Nothing is cut; three things are sequenced. **Studio Day is at full intensity in 2026–27**, so the gap cannot be closed by borrowing Sundays.

## 9 · What not to do

- **Do not add a sixth commitment.** Your own documents set the rule of scale at two commitments beside the job. This strategy adds nothing; it only sequences.
- **Do not build a new site or a new repository to "tie it together."** Three links on the existing front door do that.
- **Do not publish anything about students, clients or a real accommodation case** to evidence the adjudication line. Invented cases, clearly marked, or nothing.
- **Do not wait for Leeds to apply for roles**, and do not wait for the corpus paper to apply to Leeds. Each gate reads what exists on the day.
- **Do not let the open work become unpaid consulting.** Open the resources; price the method.
