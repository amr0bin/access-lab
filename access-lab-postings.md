## The adjudication gap, and three ways to close it without clients

*Corrected 19 September 2026.* The record shows accommodation-facing practice across seven settings (see `all-landscape-front-facing-work-and-proposed-nodes.md` in the eligibility repository). What it lacks is adjudication: reading documentation and deciding, on the institution's behalf, whether a person is entitled to accommodation. That cannot be evidenced by publishing, and it must never be evidenced with anyone's real case. The detailed route is in `competitiveness-plan-accessibility-advising.md` and `two-year-front-facing-experience-plan`, which govern. These three moves are the ones that fit a fourth Tuesday.

**1. Ask the people who do it.** A short informational conversation with an advisor at UBC's Centre for Accessibility, UBC's Centre for Workplace Accessibility, or SFU's Centre for Accessible Learning. One question: *what did the strongest candidate you've seen have that others didn't?* This is relational ledger work, it is free reconnaissance while nothing is at stake, and it is how a candidate becomes someone already known in the field.

**2. Turn the essential-requirements work toward the advisor's desk.** The hardest call an accommodation advisor makes is where a requested accommodation meets an essential requirement of a course or programme. The essential-requirements corpus study is already about exactly that. A resource that walks through the interactive process on **invented** cases, showing how essential requirements are identified and tested before accommodation is refused, is a corpus entry, a scholarly entry and a direct answer to the adjudication line. `Accommodation Practice and the Interactive Process.pdf` and `Essential Requirements Academic Standards and Assessment Validity.pdf` in this project are the starting reading. Mark it plainly as illustrative, with no case drawn from any real person.

**3. Get supervised hours in an advising role.** Crisis-line shifts, already in Stage one, supply trauma-informed advising under supervision. Beyond that, look for term, auxiliary or assistant roles in the same offices, since the UBC posting itself was a term appointment; a term role inside the office is a stronger file than any resource outside it. Advocacy volunteering with a BC disability organisation is a second route (**verify** what is available and whether it involves individual advocacy).

## The watch

Every first Tuesday of the month.

**Where:** UBC staff jobs (Workday), SFU careers, a11yjobs.com. Set saved-search alerts on all three.

**Search terms**, because titles differ by institution: Accessibility Advisor · Disability Access Advisor · Accommodation Advisor · Accessibility Coordinator · Accessibility Specialist · Centre for Accessibility · Centre for Accessible Learning · Centre for Workplace Accessibility · Disability Resource Centre (UBC Okanagan).

**What the two data points suggest:** both advisor postings seen so far appeared in spring (UBC closed 7 May 2026; SFU posted 15 May 2025). Two postings are not a cycle, so treat it as a hint, keep watching all year, and have the CV and a cover letter base current by March.
