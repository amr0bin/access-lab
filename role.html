04 · The RoleApply now, on the equivalence clause

The posting is public. Read it as the brief.

Studio Day has to guess at its ceiling. This one can be read today. UBC's last Accessibility Advisor posting set its minimum at a relevant degree and five to six years of related experience, or an equivalent combination, with a graduate degree preferred. Neither UBC's nor SFU's names the CPACC. So the MSc and the CPACC strengthen an application; they do not unlock it. Apply from Stage one, and aim at the unit, not only the title.

The gap is adjudication, and it is narrow. The record shows accommodation-facing practice across seven settings. What it does not show is deciding, on an institution's behalf, whether a person is entitled to accommodation. That line, and being known to the unit, are what everything below is for.

Five moves, in order of leverage

From competitiveness-plan-accessibility-advising.md, which governs the route. Access Lab only schedules it: most of these are fourth-Tuesday work or the one commitment outside Tuesday.

Application craft, before the next posting

  1. The CV in the postings' own vocabulary. For every line in the qualification blocks, one bullet that answers it in the same words.
  2. The equivalence paragraph, written once, well. It names the clause and makes the case, and it is reused for every application.
  3. One reference from inside disability services, asked after the conversations, of whoever was most generous.
  4. The related-experience question answered before it is asked: what accommodation-facing work has been done, and then that the rest of the practice is the same reasoning at a different scale.
  5. Over the past decade, not fifteen years. Lead with the last three years; make the newest evidence the most accommodation-facing.

First Tuesday of every month, weekly from March to June. UBC staff jobs (Workday), SFU careers, the six colleges, and a11yjobs.com, with saved alerts on each. Titles differ by institution, so search all of these:

The honest odds

An advisor-level hire from outside the unit, without direct accommodation experience, is uncommon. An operational or term role inside a unit, for someone with the CPACC, a published accessibility practice, a committee seat and a decade of adult learning design, is a reasonable expectation within a year. From inside, the advisor role is the ordinary next step.

Excellent, here

Hired as someone the unit already knows, whose file answers the panel's one doubt before it is asked, and who has published on the problem the office finds hardest. And, once in the role: the advisor whose design work reduces how many individual accommodations are needed, without ever treating individual accommodation as lesser. Individual accommodation is a right and a skilled professional practice.
