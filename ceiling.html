The Ceiling2026 to 2036

What excellent would be

Studio Day runs ten years toward a doctoral file. Access Lab runs three to four toward a job and a degree, which makes it tighter, more concrete and easier to finish. This page sets out three levels for each goal: met is what the gate requires, strong is what a prepared candidate brings, and excellent is what makes a reader remember the file. Excellent is the target. Met is the floor that never slips.

Excellence at the level of the field

What the people in Canadian post-secondary accessibility whom others cite have in common:

  • They own a question in public, with a dated trail of answers. The question is written; it belongs on the front door of Accessible Learning Labs.
  • Their resources are used by other institutions. Adoption is the evidence, which is why the licence is CC BY or CC BY-SA and every resource is deposited and versioned.
  • They sit where accommodation meets design. Most practitioners are on one side. The rare ones hold both, with the law and the measurement reasoning between. That intersection is the positioning claim, and the corpus is its proof.
  • They are accountable to disabled people, visibly. Co-authors, paid readers, named contributors. A contributor log, not a values statement.
  • They are in the professional rooms: CADSPPE, AHEAD, CDSA, an accessibility committee, JPED and CJDS. One of each is enough to be known, and none requires an affiliation.

The five ledgers

Everything Access Lab does lands in one of five accounts. A year is good when each one gains at least one entry. This is the measure that replaces feeling, reviewed on the first Tuesday of November.

ScholarlyThe four-literature paper, the corpus study, Leeds assessments, the Applied ProjectOne piece submitted, or one module passed

Accumulation, not intensity. A year in which all five gained one small entry is on the trajectory. A heroic month that produced nothing recorded is not.
