Everything made here is meant to leave

A resource an accessibility office can adopt is a contribution that runs without me, and adoption is also the evidence a panel, a supervisor and a funder read. This page is the door between this private repository and Accessible Learning Labs: what goes out, where it lands, under which licence, and what has to be true before it does.

Moving something from private to public is a decision made once and dated in the record. A plan becomes a product when it is written for its reader: the corpus design becomes a product when it is preregistered; the landscape evidence becomes one when the practised-in lines are confirmed and rendered on the map; the postings analysis becomes one when it is rewritten as what BC universities ask of the people who decide accommodation.

Three questions before anything leaves

  1. Do I own it? Section 13(3) of the Copyright Act gives an employer first ownership of work made in the course of employment. Own time, own equipment, public sources, and no employer artifact ever used as a starting point, with a dated record showing it.
  2. Is anyone else in it? A client organisation, a student, a colleague, a participant. If so, their written consent governs, obtained before. No real accommodation case, ever, even de-identified. Invented cases are marked as invented.
  3. What should happen to it? Two settings: the licence, which says what people may do, and the visibility, which says whether they get the file. A deposit cannot be undone.

If any answer is unclear, nothing is deposited. The full reasoning is in record-and-rights-across-the-portfolio.md in the project.

The licence, and why it inverts here

Everywhere else in the portfolio the text licence is restrictive. Here it is CC BY, or CC BY-SA where adaptations should stay open. An accessibility checklist that may not be adapted gets rewritten from scratch without credit; uptake is the point and the proof. Every published file carries the header:

© 2026 Andrea Robin. Licensed CC BY 4.0. Contains no client, participant or employer material. Deposited: [DOI or "not deposited"].

Open the resources, price the method

Give away what should travel. Charge for the rare skill that makes it travel well: the three engagements (accessibility review, course and curriculum design, facilitation and capacity building) stay priced as a specialism. Open work never becomes unpaid consulting.

From Tuesday to public, one resource at a time

The steps every resource goes through. Work down the list on the second Tuesday of the month.

  1. 1
    Draft, with its first-drafted date and evidence logged in the resource log the same day.
  2. 2
    Read by a disabled practitioner, paid, credited as they wish. Their changes are taken seriously, not noted.
  3. 3
    Say which it is: quality assurance or research, under TCPS 2. Anything that generalises about students or participants needs consent, ethics and a position on who owns the evidence first.
  4. 4
    The rights header and the licence, at the top of the file.
  5. 5
    Deposit on Zenodo, signed in through ORCID. Cite the concept DOI in anything durable; the version DOI when a specific text matters. A guide on its fifth version, all five dated, is stronger than a fifth version alone.
  6. 6
    Publish on accessiblelearninglabs.com, and change the landscape node's status to Published resource.
  7. 7
    Tell people: a Fieldwork note, and a direct email to one access office that might adopt it.
  8. 8
    Record it: a corpus ledger entry, and the DOI in identifiers.md.
