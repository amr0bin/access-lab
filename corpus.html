What a programme says a body must do, and whether it has to

Every Canadian programme leading to a regulated profession publishes a document saying what an applicant must be able to do. Forty to sixty of them, read against the same seven questions, answer one thing nobody has answered systematically in Canada: do these documents describe the capability a standard measures, or one particular body's way of performing it?

Why this is the flagship

If only one piece of independent work gets done well before September 2027, it is this one, because it is the only piece that serves every goal at once.

It servesBecause
The RoleThe hardest call an accommodation advisor makes is where a request meets an essential requirement: bona fide, or inherited convention (Meiorin)? This is public, methodical reasoning about exactly that decision, without claiming casework not done.
LeedsIt makes the application a practitioner-researcher's. It becomes the sampling frame for the Applied Project and feeds the Global Challenges and Building Enabling Futures assessments.
CPACCDomain Three, applied. A small overlap, and a real one.
Accessible Learning LabsA programme asking are our standards defensible? can be answered with comparative data. An open dataset and coding manual are cited more than any single finding.
The ethicThe second coder can be a disabled practitioner, paid and offered co-authorship. The same person can be Access Lab's named reader: one relationship, both roles.

The question

How do Canadian professional programmes state their essential requirements: as the constructs a standard genuinely measures, or as particular bodily methods of performing them? And what does the difference imply for who can enter the profession?

Underneath it, a quieter one: what authority do these documents claim, and has anyone ever been asked to defend them?

Why it needs no institution

TCPS 2 (2022), Article 2.2, exempts research that relies only on publicly available information with no reasonable expectation of privacy. Published admissions documents are exactly that. No ethics board certifies the exemption in advance for an unaffiliated researcher, so the reasoning is stated in the methods section and no approval is claimed. The moment interviews are added, the study needs an institution, which is what the Leeds Applied Project is for.

Pilot on twenty, then stop and revise. Code 1 will probably need subcategories and code 3 a fifth value; the frame is revised and the coding manual written before the rest are coded.

The eight stages

Stages, not dates. If one takes three months and the next six, the order holds. Stages 1 to 4 happen before September 2027; 5 to 8 run alongside the first year of the MSc.

The paper that comes first

Decided by the reasoning of 7 September 2026: the seven codes trace to four literatures, not to Fieldwork's three pillars. So the four-literature framework paper precedes the corpus study and goes to the Canadian Journal of Disability Studies (under 6,000 words, under 40 references). Its argument in one line: a defensible essential requirement must survive construct validity, the duty to accommodate, disability studies' whose body?, and programme design, and no institutional role requires more than two of these, which is why requirements that fail go unexamined. The three-pillars paper belongs to Fieldwork, on its own timetable, and later frames the Leeds interview study.

Before claiming originality, read the UK's competence-standards provision under the Equality Act 2010. If the framework already exists there, the claim becomes translation rather than origination, which is still worth making and has to be known. The CPACC's week 10 covers the same Act.

How I would know it is wrong

  1. The knowledge may exist unpublished. Ask three experienced advisors how they handle an essential-requirements dispute and listen for whether the four literatures are already in the room. These are the same three conversations on The Role.
  2. The UK may have got there first. Read the competence-standards literature before claiming origination.
  3. No competition can mean no market. If the contribution is scholarly rather than commercial, that is a fine answer, and it should be known.
