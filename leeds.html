Nine modules, one question

The online MSc Disability Studies, Rights and Inclusion at the University of Leeds, from the School of Sociology and Social Policy and its Centre for Disability Studies. The question every piece of it takes an angle on is already written: inclusive adult learning as the implementation of disability rights: how learning design in education and work decides who is included.

The rule for every assessment

Every assessment, where the brief allows, takes one angle on the question. And within a month of submitting it, it becomes a public artifact: a practitioner guide, a briefing, a Fieldwork piece.

An essay written for marks and then rewritten for practice costs one extra weekend and produces two ledger entries from one piece of thinking. After two years that is a body of published work that happened also to earn a degree. That conversion is what separates the strong version of this degree from the excellent one.

What excellent looks like here

Admission: applying as a practitioner-researcher, with a preregistered study, a published resource, referees who have read the work, a funding application in, and a named partner for the Applied Project. The programme: every assessment converted, the Applied Project run with ethics approval and disabled co-researchers, and a finish at the level the doctoral routes need, typically a distinction (verify Leeds's classification rules).
