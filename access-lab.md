## What Access Lab is for

Studio Day answers *what have you actually done?* for an admissions committee around 2034. Access Lab answers the same question for three closer readers:

1. **IAAP**, which reads an exam: the CPACC.
2. **The Leeds admissions team**, which reads a degree, a statement, references and evidence of engagement with disability studies: entry to the online MSc Disability Studies, Rights and Inclusion.
3. **A hiring panel at UBC or SFU**, which reads a CV, a file of practised work and an interview: an Accessibility Advisor post.

The first two are gates. The third is the ceiling. All three are served by the same Tuesdays.

## Three things the checking changed

These alter the plan as it stood in the pattern note and the spine, so they come first.

**1. The ceiling is closer than the plan assumed.** The UBC Accessibility Advisor posting (closed 7 May 2026) sets its *minimum* at an undergraduate degree in a relevant discipline and five to six years of related experience, or an equivalent combination. A graduate degree is *preferred*, not required. Neither the UBC nor the SFU posting mentions the CPACC. So the MSc and the CPACC strengthen an application; they do not unlock it. **Apply to the next suitable posting at either university, from Stage one, without waiting for Leeds.** This matches the spine's ring one: any role inside UBC or SFU outranks better-paid work elsewhere.

**2. The gap is adjudication, and it is narrow.** Both postings are, at their core, accommodation determination: assessing documentation, deciding eligibility, facilitating reasonable accommodation under the duty to accommodate. *Corrected 19 September 2026, after reading `all-landscape-front-facing-work-and-proposed-nodes.md`:* the record shows accommodation-facing practice across seven settings (Emily Carr, UBC Work Learn, ICBC, TRIUMF, Lighthouse Labs, Science World and ArtStarts, direct care). What it does not show is deciding, on an institution's behalf, whether a person is entitled to accommodation. That one line is what `competitiveness-plan-accessibility-advising.md` and `two-year-front-facing-experience-plan` are built to close, and they govern the route. Access Lab schedules them.

**3. The MSc is roughly twenty hours a week, not a Tuesday.** Leeds describes each module as about ten hours of tutor-guided learning plus ten of self-guided study a week, for eight weeks, with a weekly scheduled webinar. One Tuesday covers about a third of that. From the start of the MSc, Access Lab has to expand by roughly twelve hours a week, or the programme has to be paced with breaks between modules (which Leeds permits). **This is a cross-property decision about time and money, so it belongs in the main project, and it needs making before the application is submitted, not after the offer.**

## The Tuesday

### Until the CPACC is passed (22 September to 8 December 2026)

The spine's routine says 45 minutes of CPACC daily. That is about 63 hours over twelve weeks. A Tuesday-only plan must match that or it is a slower plan wearing the same name. So:

| Time | What | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 10:00–14:30 | **CPACC**, on the twelve-week plan | 12 × 4.5 h = 54 h |
| Other days | **15 minutes of recall** (flashcards or ten practice questions), six days | 12 × 1.5 h = 18 h. Spacing matters for a 100-question recall exam in a way that one long block cannot supply |
| 14:30–15:30 | **One Fieldwork move**, fortnightly until the exam; the rotation takes this hour on the other weeks | Protects the CPACC without stalling the series (`access-lab-strategy.md`, section 6) |
| 15:30–17:30 | **The rotation** (below) | The ledgers |
| 17:30–18:00 | **The record** — log line, any ledger entry, dated clean-room note | The only part that cannot be done late |

Total CPACC time ≈ 72 hours, slightly above the daily plan. `cpacc-twelve-weeks.md` is named in the project setup pack but is **not in this project's files**. Recover it from the main project or rebuild it from `CPACCBoK.pdf` on the first Tuesday.

### After the CPACC, until the MSc

| 12:00–13:30 | **One Fieldwork move** |
| 13:30–15:30 | **The four-literature paper** until submitted, then the corpus study again |

| **Scholarly** | The three-pillars conceptual paper, the essential-requirements corpus study, Leeds assessments, the Applied Project | One piece submitted, or one MSc module passed |
| **Relational** | Disabled practitioners and community organisations; advisors at UBC's Centre for Accessibility and Centre for Workplace Accessibility and SFU's CAL; Leeds staff; the IAAP community | One relationship begun and one kept warm |

**The review.** First Tuesday of November, every year, one question per ledger: *did it gain an entry?* Baseline on 3 November 2026, so the first real review on 2 November 2027 has something to compare against. The template is in `access-lab-record.md`.

**Accumulation, not intensity.** A year in which all five gained one small entry is on the trajectory. A heroic month that produced nothing recorded is not.

## The question

One dated paragraph a year, kept in `access-lab-record.md`, answering: *what has the three-pillars hypothesis found this year?*

The working hypothesis, from Fieldwork: a learning environment needs accessibility for the legal and technical floor, disability justice for whose knowledge shapes it, and emergent learning to keep it able to change, and where policy and practice diverge, one of the three is missing. The annual paragraph is not a restatement of that. It is what the year's work showed about it: where it held, where it failed, what it could not see. Ten dated versions are a trajectory, and the 2027 version is the spine of the Leeds statement.

## The named reader

A programme supplies a critic. Alone, one has to be recruited, and in this property the ethic decides who.

**A disabled accessibility practitioner, paid, reading one piece a quarter.** Not a friend doing a favour: *nothing free that should be paid* and *nothing about us without us* point the same way here. Ideally someone who has done accommodation casework in BC post-secondary, because that reader also closes the gap the postings expose.

From the MSc, the Leeds tutors and then the Applied Project supervisor add a second, institutional reader. They do not replace the first.

## The governing ethic

Disabled people are involved in producing the resources, credited by name, and paid. A body of accessibility work made without that is worth less than nothing at a UBC or SFU hiring table. The contributor log in `access-lab-record.md` exists so that this is a record, not a claim.

Two standing rules from the setup pack apply every Tuesday: say whether a piece is **quality assurance or research** under TCPS 2, and never let a claim about students go public before consent, FIPPA and ownership of the evidence are settled.
